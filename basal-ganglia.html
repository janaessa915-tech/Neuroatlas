Basal Ganglia

العقد القاعدية

The basal ganglia are a group of interconnected deep brain nuclei that play an important role in movement control, action selection, procedural learning, and habit formation.

📍 Location

The basal ganglia are located deep within the cerebral hemispheres and are closely connected with the cerebral cortex, thalamus, and brainstem.

🚶 Movement Control

Basal ganglia circuits help select and regulate voluntary movements rather than directly producing the movement itself.

🧠 Major Structures

Important components include the caudate nucleus, putamen, globus pallidus, subthalamic nucleus, and substantia nigra.

🔗 Neural Circuits

The basal ganglia participate in loops connecting the cortex, basal ganglia, thalamus, and cortex, allowing information to be processed through interconnected circuits.

🧪 Learning & Habits

Basal ganglia circuits contribute to procedural learning, reinforcement-based learning, and the development of repeated behaviors and habits.

🩺 Clinical Relevance

Disruption of basal ganglia circuits is associated with movement disorders such as Parkinson disease and Huntington disease.

NeuroAtlas Insight

The basal ganglia are not simply a "movement center." They are part of several parallel circuits that help the brain select actions, regulate movement, and learn from experience.
