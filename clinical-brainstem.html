Brainstem
Localization

تحديد موضع الآفة في جذع الدماغ

The brainstem is one of the most powerful regions for neurological localization because it contains cranial nerve nuclei, long ascending sensory pathways, descending motor pathways, and connections with the cerebellum. The most important clue is often the relationship between the face, cranial nerves, and the body.

جذع الدماغ من أهم المناطق في تحديد موضع الآفة العصبية، لأنه يحتوي على أنوية الأعصاب القحفية والمسارات الحركية والحسية الصاعدة والنازلة، بالإضافة إلى الاتصالات المهمة مع المخيخ. ومن أهم العلامات التي تساعدنا على تحديد المكان وجود أعراض في أحد الأعصاب القحفية مع أعراض في الجانب المقابل من الجسم.

CASE 01 · MIDBRAIN

The Drooping Eyelid

تدلي الجفن واضطراب حركة العين

A 59-year-old man develops sudden double vision and difficulty keeping his right eye open. His family also notices weakness on the left side of his body.

AGE 59 years
ONSET Sudden
CRANIAL NERVE CN III pattern
BODY FINDING Left-sided weakness

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient was walking normally earlier in the morning. He suddenly developed double vision followed by a drooping right eyelid. When asked to look in different directions, movements of the right eye are abnormal. Several minutes later, his family notices weakness in the left arm and left leg.

كان المريض يمشي بصورة طبيعية في بداية اليوم. ظهرت لديه فجأة ازدواجية في الرؤية ثم بدأ الجفن الأيمن في التدلي. عند فحص حركة العين، توجد صعوبة في تحريك العين اليمنى في عدة اتجاهات. وبعد دقائق لاحظت أسرته ضعفًا في الذراع والساق اليسرى.

Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we find?

01 · EYE MOVEMENT

Right Oculomotor Dysfunction

The right eye has impaired movements consistent with involvement of the oculomotor nerve pathway.

Clue: a cranial nerve finding localizes the lesion to a specific brainstem region.
02 · EYELID

Right Ptosis

The right upper eyelid is drooping.

Clue: CN III dysfunction can produce ptosis.
03 · MOTOR

Left Arm + Leg Weakness

Strength is reduced on the left side of the body.

Clue: the body deficit is on the opposite side from the cranial nerve finding.
04 · SENSORY

No Major Sensory Level

There is no clear sensory level suggesting a spinal cord lesion.

Clue: the pattern is strongly brainstem-oriented.

CASE 02 · PONS

The Face-Arm Cross

ضعف الوجه مع ضعف الجانب المقابل من الجسم

A 64-year-old woman suddenly develops weakness of the right side of her face. At the same time, her left arm and leg become weak. She also complains of double vision.

AGE 64 years
ONSET Sudden
CRANIAL NERVE CN VI + VII pattern
BODY FINDING Left hemiparesis

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient was previously independent. While speaking with her daughter, she suddenly noticed that the room appeared to move when she looked toward the left. Her daughter then noticed that the right side of her face looked weak. Within minutes, weakness of the left arm and leg became apparent.

كانت المريضة مستقلة في حياتها اليومية. أثناء حديثها مع ابنتها، شعرت فجأة باضطراب في الرؤية عند النظر إلى اليسار. لاحظت ابنتها وجود ضعف في الجانب الأيمن من الوجه، وبعد دقائق ظهر ضعف في الذراع والساق اليسرى.

Sudden visual disturbance begins.
11:23
Right facial weakness becomes noticeable.
11:27
Left arm weakness develops.
11:30
Left leg weakness is confirmed.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we find?

01 · EYE MOVEMENT

Abnormal Horizontal Gaze

The patient has difficulty coordinating horizontal eye movement, especially when looking toward the affected side.

Clue: horizontal gaze abnormalities can point toward pontine structures.
02 · FACE

Right Facial Weakness

The weakness involves the right side of the face.

Clue: a peripheral-pattern facial deficit can indicate involvement of the facial nerve nucleus or fascicles.
03 · BODY

Left Arm + Leg Weakness

The left upper and lower limbs are weak.

Clue: the body deficit is opposite the facial deficit.
04 · SENSATION

Mild Left-Sided Sensory Change

Sensation is mildly reduced on the left side of the body.

Clue: ascending sensory pathways may also be involved.

CASE 03 · MEDULLA

The Swallowing Problem

اضطراب البلع والصوت مع علامات حسية

A 71-year-old man develops sudden difficulty swallowing, a hoarse voice, dizziness, and sensory changes affecting different sides of the face and body.

AGE 71 years
ONSET Sudden
KEY CRANIAL NERVE CN IX / X pattern
KEY FEATURE Crossed sensory findings

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient suddenly becomes dizzy while standing. He feels that the room is spinning and has difficulty walking. Soon afterward, his voice becomes hoarse and he has difficulty swallowing liquids. He also describes reduced sensation over one side of his face and altered pain and temperature sensation over the opposite side of his body.

شعر المريض فجأة بدوار شديد أثناء الوقوف، وأصبح يشعر بأن المكان يدور من حوله مع صعوبة في المشي. بعد ذلك أصبح صوته أجش وظهرت لديه صعوبة في بلع السوائل. كما لاحظ انخفاض الإحساس في أحد جانبي الوجه مع تغير الإحساس بالألم والحرارة في الجانب المقابل من الجسم.

Sudden vertigo begins.
15:10
Gait becomes markedly unsteady.
15:15

Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we find?

01 · BULBAR

Dysphagia + Hoarseness

The patient has difficulty swallowing and a hoarse voice.

Clue: these findings can suggest involvement of lower cranial nerve pathways.
02 · FACE SENSATION

Reduced Facial Pain/Temperature

Pain and temperature sensation are reduced on one side of the face.

Clue: trigeminal sensory pathways may be involved.
03 · BODY SENSATION

Opposite Body Sensory Change

Pain and temperature sensation are reduced on the opposite side of the body.

Clue: crossed sensory findings are important for brainstem localization.
04 · COORDINATION

Marked Ataxia

The patient has difficulty maintaining balance and walks with a broad-based, unsteady gait.

Clue: nearby cerebellar connections may be involved.
05 · AUTONOMIC

Possible Ipsilateral Horner Pattern

There is subtle ptosis and pupillary asymmetry on the affected side.

Clue: descending sympathetic fibers can pass through the lateral brainstem.
06 · MOTOR

No Major Hemiparesis

Strength is relatively preserved compared with the prominent sensory, bulbar, vestibular, and coordination findings.

Clue: this helps distinguish the pattern from a major corticospinal lesion.

01 · Recognize the pattern

The combination of vertigo, dysphagia, hoarseness, crossed sensory findings, and ataxia suggests a lateral brainstem syndrome.

Sensory Crossed face/body findings
Coordination Ataxia + vertigo

02 · Identify the cranial nerve region

Dysphagia and hoarseness suggest involvement of the nucleus ambiguus region, associated with CN IX and X functions.

CN IX Glossopharyngeal functions
CN X Pharyngeal/laryngeal functions
Key clue Hoarseness + dysphagia

03 · Analyze the sensory pattern

Loss of facial pain and temperature on one side with loss of body pain and temperature on the opposite side is a classic crossed sensory brainstem pattern.

Face Ipsilateral sensory change
Body Contralateral sensory change
Meaning Brainstem localization

04 · Connect the additional systems

Vertigo can reflect vestibular pathway involvement, while ataxia can reflect disruption of cerebellar connections. A Horner pattern can occur when descending sympathetic fibers are affected.

Vestibular Vertigo
Cerebellar connections Ataxia
Sympathetic Possible Horner pattern

05 · Final localization

The combined findings strongly support a lateral medullary localization. Multiple systems are involved because the lateral medulla contains or lies near several ascending, descending, cranial nerve, vestibular, and cerebellar pathways.

Level Medulla
Region Lateral medulla
Pattern Multisystem brainstem syndrome

Differential Localization · المقارنة التشريحية

Why the brainstem?

A good localization should explain why the findings fit the brainstem and why alternative levels are less convincing.

🧠 Cerebral Cortex

Cortical lesions can cause contralateral weakness, sensory changes, language deficits, visual abnormalities, or seizures.

Why less likely in these cases:
Crossed cranial-nerve and body findings are much more characteristic of brainstem localization.

🌀 Cerebellum

Cerebellar lesions can produce ataxia, dysmetria, imbalance, and coordination abnormalities.

Why not enough alone:
Cerebellar dysfunction does not explain cranial nerve deficits plus crossed sensory or motor findings by itself.

⚡ Spinal Cord

Spinal lesions can produce motor and sensory deficits below a specific level.

Why less likely:
The presence of cranial nerve findings points above the spinal cord and toward the brainstem.

🎯 NeuroAtlas Brainstem Takeaway

The brainstem is especially useful for localization because multiple systems are packed into a small anatomical space. A cranial nerve finding on one side combined with a motor or sensory deficit on the opposite side should immediately make you consider a brainstem lesion. The exact cranial nerve involved can then help determine the level: midbrain, pons, or medulla.


جذع الدماغ مهم جدًا في تحديد موضع الآفة لأن العديد من المسارات والأعصاب القحفية تمر في مساحة صغيرة. وجود علامة في عصب قحفي في جانب مع وجود ضعف أو اضطراب حسي في الجانب المقابل يجب أن يجعلنا نفكر في آفة في جذع الدماغ. بعد ذلك يمكن استخدام العصب القحفي المتأثر لتحديد المستوى: الدماغ الأوسط أو الجسر أو النخاع المستطيل.
