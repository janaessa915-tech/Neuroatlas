تحديد موضع الآفة في المخيخ

Cerebellar localization is not simply about recognizing the word "ataxia." The distribution of coordination problems, gait findings, eye movements, speech, tremor, and postural control can help identify which cerebellar system is involved.

CASE 01 · CEREBELLAR HEMISPHERE

The Unsteady Right Hand

اليد اليمنى غير المتناسقة

A 54-year-old woman develops difficulty performing precise movements with her right hand over several weeks. She reports dropping objects, missing targets when reaching, and feeling "clumsy" despite normal strength.

AGE 54 years
ONSET Several weeks
MAIN PROBLEM Right arm coordination
STRENGTH Preserved

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient describes increasing difficulty using her right hand for tasks requiring accuracy. She has trouble reaching for a cup without overshooting it and occasionally knocks objects over. She does not describe numbness or loss of strength.

تصف المريضة زيادة تدريجية في صعوبة استخدام يدها اليمنى في الحركات التي تحتاج إلى دقة. تجد صعوبة في الوصول إلى الكوب دون تجاوز الهدف، وأحيانًا تسقط الأشياء أو تصطدم بها. ولا تصف وجود فقدان للإحساس أو ضعف حقيقي في القوة.

WEEK 1
Patient notices occasional clumsiness of the right hand.
WEEK 2
Difficulty accurately reaching for small objects becomes more obvious.
WEEK 3
Family notices an irregular tremor during intentional movement.
TODAY
Neurological examination demonstrates a clear right-sided coordination syndrome.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What does the examination reveal?

01 · STRENGTH

Normal Power

Strength is 5/5 in the right and left upper limbs.

Clue: poor movement accuracy is not explained by weakness.
02 · FINGER-NOSE

Right Dysmetria

The patient's right index finger repeatedly overshoots the target.

Clue: dysmetria is a classic cerebellar sign.
03 · RAPID MOVEMENTS

Right Dysdiadochokinesia

Rapid alternating movements of the right hand are irregular and slow.

Clue: impaired rapid alternating movement suggests cerebellar dysfunction.
04 · REFLEXES

No Major Pyramidal Pattern

There is no prominent spasticity or pathologic plantar response.

Clue: the examination does not strongly suggest a corticospinal lesion.
05 · SENSATION

Intact Sensation

Light touch and proprioception are preserved.

Clue: sensory loss is not driving the abnormal movement.
06 · GAIT

Mild Right-Sided Ataxia

The patient has mild instability with tandem walking but no major weakness.

Clue: limb coordination and gait abnormalities support cerebellar involvement.

CASE 02 · VERMIS / MIDLINE

The Unsteady Walk

اضطراب المشي والتحكم في الجذع

A 61-year-old man develops progressive difficulty walking. He repeatedly feels as though he is falling to either side and has difficulty maintaining a stable upright posture.

AGE 61 years
MAIN PROBLEM Gait instability
UPPER LIMBS Relatively preserved
KEY DOMAIN Truncal coordination

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient reports that his walking has gradually become wider and less stable. He needs to hold onto furniture when turning. His family notices that he sways while standing and occasionally loses his balance even when his feet are close together.

يذكر المريض أن مشيته أصبحت تدريجيًا أكثر اتساعًا وأقل ثباتًا. ويحتاج إلى الإمساك بالأثاث عند الدوران. كما لاحظت أسرته أنه يتمايل أثناء الوقوف وأحيانًا يفقد توازنه حتى عندما تكون قدماه قريبتين من بعضهما.

MONTH 1
Patient notices increased instability while walking.
MONTH 2
Broad-based gait becomes obvious to family members.
MONTH 3
Difficulty maintaining a stable seated and standing posture develops.
TODAY
Neurological examination confirms prominent truncal and gait ataxia.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What pattern is emerging?

01 · GAIT

Broad-Based Gait

The patient walks with his feet widely separated to maintain stability.

Clue: a broad-based gait is a major cerebellar localization clue.
02 · TRUNK

Truncal Ataxia

The patient sways significantly while sitting and standing.

Clue: prominent truncal instability raises suspicion for midline cerebellar involvement.
03 · LIMBS

Mild Limb Findings

Finger-nose testing is only mildly abnormal bilaterally.

Clue: gait and trunk dysfunction dominate the presentation.
04 · STRENGTH

Preserved Strength

No major weakness is found in the upper or lower limbs.

Clue: instability is not simply caused by loss of muscle power.
05 · SENSATION

No Clear Sensory Level

Proprioception and light touch are preserved.

Clue: a sensory ataxia pattern is less likely.
06 · SPEECH

Mild Scanning Quality

Speech is slightly irregular and poorly timed.

Clue: cerebellar networks can influence speech timing and coordination.

The major abnormalities are broad-based gait, truncal sway, difficulty maintaining posture, and mild bilateral limb incoordination.

The dominant system is postural and axial coordination. This pattern is more characteristic of midline cerebellar systems than a purely unilateral limb syndrome.

03 · Recognize the distribution

The most severe abnormalities involve standing, walking, and trunk control rather than one upper limb. This distribution is important because different cerebellar regions contribute differently to axial and appendicular coordination.

04 · Determine the level

The examination supports cerebellar involvement. The preserved strength and sensation make a primary corticospinal or sensory lesion less convincing as the main explanation.

05 · Narrow the region

Prominent truncal and gait ataxia points toward midline cerebellar structures, particularly the vermis and associated axial-control networks.

Region Cerebellar vermis

06 · Explain the entire pattern

A midline cerebellar localization provides a strong anatomical explanation for the patient's severe gait and truncal instability, with relatively mild bilateral limb findings.

CASE 03 · VESTIBULOCEREBELLAR SYSTEM

The Patient Who Cannot Keep His Eyes Still

اضطراب التوازن وحركات العين

A 47-year-old woman presents with dizziness, imbalance, and difficulty keeping her vision stable when turning her head. Her main problem is not weakness but the coordination of eye movements and balance.

AGE 47 years
MAIN PROBLEM Dizziness + imbalance
VISION Oscillopsia
KEY DOMAIN Eye + balance control

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient reports that the environment seems to move or bounce when she walks. Turning her head makes the sensation worse. She has difficulty maintaining a stable visual target while moving and feels particularly unsteady in visually complex environments.

تذكر المريضة أن البيئة المحيطة تبدو وكأنها تتحرك أو تهتز أثناء المشي. ويزداد الإحساس عند تحريك الرأس. كما تجد صعوبة في الحفاظ على ثبات الصورة أثناء الحركة وتشعر بعدم الثبات بشكل أكبر في الأماكن المزدحمة بصريًا.

DAY 1
Patient develops dizziness and visual instability.
DAY 3
Walking becomes difficult, especially while turning the head.
DAY 5
Oscillopsia becomes the dominant complaint.
TODAY
Examination demonstrates abnormal ocular motor control and balance.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

Eye movements change the localization

01 · OCULAR MOTOR

Abnormal Smooth Pursuit

Eye movements used to follow a moving target are irregular rather than smoothly coordinated.

Clue: cerebellar networks contribute to precise ocular motor control.
02 · NYSTAGMUS

Gaze-Evoked Nystagmus

The patient develops rhythmic involuntary eye movements when looking away from the center.

Clue: abnormal eye movement patterns can provide important localization clues.
03 · BALANCE

Postural Instability

The patient is unstable while standing and becomes worse with head movement.

Clue: vestibular-cerebellar systems help integrate balance information.
04 · LIMBS

Minimal Limb Dysmetria

Finger-nose testing produces only subtle abnormalities.

Clue: the limb coordination deficit is not the dominant feature.
05 · STRENGTH

Normal Strength

There is no major weakness in the upper or lower limbs.

Clue: the primary problem is coordination and balance.
06 · SENSATION

Sensation Preserved

Light touch and proprioception are not significantly impaired.

Clue: the symptoms cannot be easily explained by sensory loss.

This case demonstrates why cerebellar localization is broader than limb coordination alone. Eye movements and balance can be equally important.

01 · Identify the findings

The dominant abnormalities are ocular motor dysfunction, nystagmus, postural instability, and oscillopsia.

02 · Identify the functional system

The pattern strongly involves vestibular and ocular motor coordination rather than primary muscle strength.

Eye control Abnormal
Balance Abnormal
Strength Preserved

03 · Recognize the pattern

The combination of dizziness, visual instability, abnormal nystagmus, and postural imbalance suggests dysfunction of systems that integrate vestibular information with eye and postural control.

04 · Determine the level

The neurological pattern is compatible with cerebellar involvement, particularly the systems that communicate with vestibular pathways and regulate ocular and postural responses.

05 · Narrow the region

Vestibulocerebellar structures, particularly the flocculonodular lobe and associated midline networks, are important for equilibrium and eye movement control.

06 · Explain the entire pattern

The ocular motor abnormalities, oscillopsia, and postural instability can be unified by dysfunction of vestibulocerebellar circuits involved in integrating vestibular signals with eye and postural control.

Clinical Integration · الربط السريري

Three patterns. Three localization strategies.

The cerebellum should not be treated as one uniform structure. The clinical pattern can help you decide whether the dominant problem involves limb coordination, axial control, or vestibular/ocular function.

🧠 Cerebellar Hemisphere

Think about ipsilateral limb dysmetria, dysdiadochokinesia, intention tremor, and impaired precision.

Case 01:
Right limb coordination → right cerebellar hemisphere.

🌀 Vermis / Midline

Think about gait instability, truncal ataxia, and impaired axial postural control.

Case 02:
Prominent trunk and gait dysfunction → midline cerebellar systems.

👁️ Vestibulocerebellar System

Think about equilibrium, nystagmus, ocular motor abnormalities, and visual instability during movement.

Case 03:
Eye + balance dysfunction → vestibulocerebellar circuits.

🎯 NeuroAtlas Clinical Takeaway

Cerebellar localization depends heavily on the distribution of the coordination deficit. A unilateral limb syndrome can point toward a cerebellar hemisphere, prominent truncal and gait ataxia can suggest midline cerebellar structures, and dominant ocular or vestibular findings can suggest vestibulocerebellar circuits.
