CASE 01 · DOMINANT FRONTAL CORTEX

The Sudden Neurological Deficit

الحالة العصبية المفاجئة

A 68-year-old right-handed man is brought to the emergency department because his family noticed sudden difficulty moving his right arm and face approximately two hours earlier.

AGE 68 years
HANDEDNESS Right-handed
ONSET Sudden
MAIN PROBLEM Right face + arm weakness

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient was previously independent. He was having breakfast when his family noticed that his right hand suddenly became weak. He then developed difficulty smiling normally on the right side. His speech also sounded unusual, and he appeared to have trouble getting some words out.

كان المريض مستقلًا في حياته اليومية قبل ذلك. أثناء تناوله الإفطار، لاحظت أسرته أن يده اليمنى أصبحت ضعيفة بشكل مفاجئ، ثم ظهرت صعوبة في الابتسام بصورة طبيعية من الجانب الأيمن. كما أصبح كلامه غير طبيعي وبدا أنه يجد صعوبة في إخراج بعض الكلمات.

08:10
Patient was normal and eating breakfast.
08:25
Sudden right hand weakness was noticed.
08:30
Right facial weakness and abnormal speech developed.
09:05
Patient arrived for neurological evaluation.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we actually find?

01 · MOTOR

Right Face + Arm Weakness

Strength is reduced in the right upper limb. The lower face is also weak on the right.

Face and arm are affected more than the leg.
02 · LANGUAGE

Expressive Difficulty

The patient understands simple questions but produces short, effortful speech.

A language deficit strongly supports cortical involvement.
03 · SENSORY

Mild Right Hand Sensory Loss

Light-touch sensation is reduced over the right hand and forearm.

Motor and sensory findings occur together.
04 · VISUAL

No Major Visual Deficit

No clear visual-field disturbance is identified on the initial examination.

The main problem is not primarily visual.
05 · COORDINATION

No Primary Cerebellar Pattern

There is no prominent dysmetria, intention tremor, or isolated coordination syndrome.

Weakness is the dominant motor problem.
06 · CRANIAL NERVES

Lower Facial Weakness

The right lower face is weak while the upper face is relatively preserved.

This is compatible with corticobulbar involvement.

CASE 02 · PARIETAL CORTEX

The Patient Who Cannot Find His Left Side

المريض الذي لا ينتبه إلى الجانب الأيسر

A 72-year-old right-handed woman is brought to clinic after her family notices that she repeatedly ignores objects on her left, bumps into doorways, and insists that there is nothing wrong with her left arm.

AGE 72 years
HANDEDNESS Right-handed
ONSET Subacute
MAIN PROBLEM Left-sided neglect

History · التاريخ المرضي

Her family reports that she has recently started eating only the food on the right side of her plate. When asked to copy a simple drawing, she reproduces only the right half. She frequently bumps into objects located on her left and becomes frustrated when family members point this out.

لاحظت الأسرة أنها بدأت مؤخرًا في تناول الطعام الموجود في الجانب الأيمن فقط من الطبق. وعندما طُلب منها نسخ رسم بسيط، قامت برسم النصف الأيمن فقط. كما تصطدم بشكل متكرر بالأشياء الموجودة على يسارها، وتشعر بالضيق عندما يخبرها أفراد الأسرة بذلك.

DAY 1
Family first notices that she leaves food on the left side of the plate.
DAY 3
She begins bumping into objects on her left.
DAY 5
She denies having difficulty using or noticing the left side.
DAY 7
Neurological examination demonstrates prominent left-sided neglect.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

Look beyond simple weakness.

01 · ATTENTION

Left Hemispatial Neglect

The patient consistently fails to attend to stimuli presented on the left side of space.

Strong clue for a right parietal attentional network.
02 · BODY AWARENESS

Reduced Awareness of Left Side

She behaves as though the left side of her body is less relevant and does not spontaneously correct errors involving it.

Suggests disturbed spatial awareness rather than simple weakness.
03 · SENSORY

Cortical Sensory Difficulty

Basic sensation is relatively preserved, but complex sensory recognition is impaired.

Higher-order sensory processing can be affected by parietal lesions.
04 · MOTOR

Mild Left Arm Weakness

There is mild weakness of the left upper limb, but it does not fully explain the patient's behavior.

The attentional deficit is much more prominent.
05 · LANGUAGE

No Major Aphasia

She follows instructions and communicates normally without a major expressive or receptive language deficit.

This makes a dominant frontal language syndrome less likely.
06 · VISUAL

Visual Field Testing

Formal testing does not show a simple isolated left homonymous visual-field deficit that explains all of her behavior.

Neglect is broader than a primary visual-field loss.

Best fit:

CASE 03 · OCCIPITAL CORTEX

The Missing Half of the Visual World

فقدان نصف المجال البصري

A 61-year-old man suddenly notices that he keeps missing objects on the right side while reading and walking. He has no significant weakness, no aphasia, and no major sensory complaint.

AGE 61 years
HANDEDNESS Right-handed
ONSET Sudden
MAIN PROBLEM Right visual-field loss

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient reports that while reading, the right side of words seems to disappear. He also misses people approaching from his right while walking. He can move both arms normally and has no difficulty understanding or producing speech.

يذكر المريض أنه أثناء القراءة يبدو أن الجانب الأيمن من الكلمات يختفي. كما أنه لا يلاحظ الأشخاص الذين يقتربون من جانبه الأيمن أثناء المشي. يستطيع تحريك ذراعيه بشكل طبيعي ولا توجد لديه صعوبة واضحة في فهم الكلام أو إنتاجه.

14:10
Patient notices missing objects on the right side.
14:30
Reading becomes difficult because the right side of text is missed.
15:05
No weakness or language deficit is identified.
15:30
Visual-field testing demonstrates a right homonymous deficit.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

Trace the visual pathway.

01 · VISUAL FIELD

Right Homonymous Field Loss

The right half of the visual field is absent in both eyes when tested appropriately.

A post-chiasmal lesion can produce a homonymous field deficit.
02 · MOTOR

Strength Preserved

Strength is normal in both upper and lower limbs.

There is no major corticospinal motor syndrome.
03 · LANGUAGE

No Aphasia

Speech production and comprehension are preserved.

The dominant language cortex is not the main problem.
04 · SENSORY

No Major Body Sensory Loss

The patient has no significant hemisensory deficit.

The deficit is highly localized to vision.
05 · EYE MOVEMENTS

Extraocular Movements Preserved

The eyes move normally without a major ocular-motor syndrome.

This supports a central visual pathway problem rather than an ocular-motor deficit.
06 · HIGHER FUNCTION

Visual Recognition Mostly Preserved

The patient recognizes familiar objects but fails to perceive the right half of the visual field.

The deficit is consistent with visual-field loss rather than generalized cognition failure.

An optic-nerve lesion typically produces monocular visual loss.

Less likely:
The pattern is homonymous rather than bitemporal.

Processes visual information from the contralateral visual field.

Best fit:
Explains an isolated contralateral homonymous field deficit.

🎯 NeuroAtlas Clinical Takeaway

These three cases demonstrate why localization depends on the pattern, not a single symptom. Case 01 uses motor distribution and language dysfunction to point toward the left dominant frontal cortex. Case 02 uses left hemispatial neglect and higher-order sensory findings to point toward the right parietal cortex. Case 03 uses a right homonymous visual-field deficit to point toward the left occipital visual cortex.


توضح الحالات الثلاث أن تحديد موضع الآفة يعتمد على نمط الأعراض وليس على عرض واحد فقط. الحالة الأولى تشير إلى القشرة الجبهية اليسرى المسيطرة من خلال الضعف الحركي واضطراب اللغة. الحالة الثانية تشير إلى القشرة الجدارية اليمنى من خلال إهمال الجانب الأيسر واضطراب الوعي المكاني. أما الحالة الثالثة فتشير إلى القشرة البصرية القذالية اليسرى من خلال فقدان المجال البصري الأيمن.
