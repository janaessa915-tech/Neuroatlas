CASE 01 · CORTEX

The Sudden Neurological Deficit

الحالة العصبية المفاجئة

A 68-year-old right-handed man is brought to the emergency department because his family noticed sudden difficulty moving his right arm and face approximately two hours earlier.

AGE 68 years
HANDEDNESS Right-handed
ONSET Sudden
MAIN PROBLEM Right-sided weakness

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient was previously independent. He was having breakfast when his family noticed that his right hand suddenly became weak. He then developed difficulty smiling normally on the right side. His speech also sounded unusual, and he appeared to have trouble getting some words out.

كان المريض مستقلًا في حياته اليومية قبل ذلك. أثناء تناوله الإفطار، لاحظت أسرته أن يده اليمنى أصبحت ضعيفة بشكل مفاجئ، ثم ظهرت صعوبة في الابتسام بصورة طبيعية من الجانب الأيمن. كما أصبح كلامه غير طبيعي وبدا أنه يجد صعوبة في إخراج بعض الكلمات.

08:10
Patient was normal and eating breakfast.
08:25
Sudden right hand weakness was noticed.
08:30
Right facial weakness and abnormal speech developed.
09:05
Patient arrived for neurological evaluation.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we actually find?

01 · MOTOR

Right Face + Arm Weakness

Strength is reduced in the right upper limb. The lower face is also weak on the right.

High-yield clue: face and arm are affected more than the leg.
02 · LANGUAGE

Expressive Difficulty

The patient understands simple questions but produces short, effortful speech.

High-yield clue: language dysfunction suggests cortical involvement.
03 · SENSORY

Mild Right Hand Sensory Loss

Light-touch sensation is reduced over the right hand and forearm.

High-yield clue: sensory findings accompany the motor deficit.
04 · VISUAL

No Major Visual Complaint

The patient does not report a clear visual-field disturbance during the initial examination.

High-yield clue: the dominant problem is not primarily visual.
05 · COORDINATION

No Primary Cerebellar Pattern

There is no prominent intention tremor, dysmetria, or isolated coordination syndrome.

High-yield clue: this does not look primarily cerebellar.
06 · CRANIAL NERVES

Lower Facial Weakness

The right lower face is weak, while the upper face is relatively preserved.

High-yield clue: corticobulbar involvement can produce this pattern.

01 · Identify the important findings

The most important abnormalities are right lower facial weakness, right upper-limb weakness, mild right upper-limb sensory loss, and difficulty producing language.

Motor Right face + arm weakness
Language Expressive difficulty
Sensory Right hand/forearm

02 · Identify the functional system

The dominant system is motor, but the presence of language dysfunction is extremely important because language is a higher cortical function.

Primary Motor system
Additional Language system
Supporting Sensory system

03 · Determine the side

The weakness is on the right side of the body. Because major descending motor pathways cross before reaching the spinal motor neurons, a supratentorial motor lesion usually produces contralateral weakness.

Weak side Right
Likely brain side Left
Key principle Contralateral motor control

04 · Determine the neurological level

The combination of a focal motor deficit with a language disturbance strongly favors a cerebral cortical or nearby supratentorial localization rather than a peripheral nerve, spinal cord, or isolated cerebellar lesion.

Cortex Strongly supported
Brainstem Less likely
Spinal / PNS Does not explain language

05 · Narrow the cortical region

Face and upper-limb weakness greater than leg involvement points toward the lateral portion of the primary motor cortex and its nearby corticobulbar representation. The language deficit further supports involvement of the dominant hemisphere.

Side Left hemisphere
Motor region Lateral precentral gyrus
Language Dominant frontal network

06 · Explain the entire pattern

The findings can be unified by a left lateral frontal cortical localization involving motor representations for the face and upper limb, with nearby dominant-hemisphere language networks also affected.

Right weakness Left motor cortex
Face + arm Lateral cortical representation
Language Dominant hemisphere involvement

Differential Localization · المقارنة التشريحية

Why not somewhere else?

Localization becomes stronger when you can explain not only why one location fits, but also why competing locations fit less well.

🧩 Brainstem

Brainstem lesions can produce cranial nerve abnormalities with long-tract findings and may create crossed face-body patterns.

Why less likely here:
The language disturbance and cortical distribution favor a supratentorial cortical process.

🌀 Cerebellum

Cerebellar dysfunction typically produces abnormalities of coordination, balance, timing, and movement accuracy.

Why less likely here:
The dominant finding is weakness rather than a primary coordination syndrome.

⚡ Peripheral Nerve

Peripheral lesions usually follow a nerve, root, plexus, or muscle distribution.

Why less likely here:
A peripheral lesion cannot easily explain the associated language dysfunction.
Clinical Reasoning Test · اختبار التفكير السريري

Can you localize it?

QUESTION 01

The patient's weakness is predominantly on which side?

Correct. The examination describes weakness of the right face and right upper limb.
QUESTION 02

Which brain hemisphere is most likely involved in a supratentorial lesion producing this motor pattern?

Correct. Major corticospinal motor pathways produce contralateral control after their relevant crossing, making a left cerebral lesion a strong localization for right-sided weakness.
QUESTION 03

Why is the language finding especially useful for localization?

Correct. Language functions depend on distributed cortical networks and are therefore powerful clues when determining cortical localization.
QUESTION 04

Face and arm weakness greater than leg weakness points most strongly toward which cortical region?

Correct. The motor homunculus places face and upper-limb representations more laterally, while the lower limb is represented more medially.
QUESTION 05

Which localization best unifies the complete pattern?

Correct. The combination of contralateral face-arm weakness, sensory findings, and language dysfunction points toward the dominant left lateral frontal cortical region.

🎯 NeuroAtlas Clinical Takeaway

Localization is a process of pattern recognition supported by anatomy. In this case, the combination of right face-arm weakness + sensory involvement + language dysfunction moves the reasoning toward the left dominant lateral cerebral cortex. The distribution of the weakness helps narrow the motor component toward the lateral motor representation.
