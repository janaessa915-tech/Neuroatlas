Motor
Localization

تحديد موضع الآفة في المسار الحركي

Motor localization is the process of using weakness, reflexes, muscle tone, distribution of affected muscles, and associated neurological findings to determine where a lesion lies along the motor system. The major possibilities include the motor cortex, corticospinal tract, brainstem, spinal cord, anterior horn cell, peripheral nerve, neuromuscular junction, and muscle.

تحديد موضع الآفة الحركية يعتمد على تحليل نمط الضعف، والمنعكسات، وتوتر العضلات، وتوزيع العضلات المتأثرة، والأعراض العصبية المصاحبة. ومن خلال هذه العلامات يمكن تحديد مكان الإصابة على طول الجهاز الحركي، من القشرة الحركية وحتى العضلة.

CASE 01 · MOTOR CORTEX

The Weak Hand

ضعف اليد مع علامات قشرية

A 67-year-old woman suddenly develops weakness mainly affecting her right hand. She also has difficulty performing a previously familiar skilled movement and has mild word-finding difficulty.

ONSET Sudden
MAIN DEFICIT Right hand weakness
ASSOCIATED Cortical signs

The patient was preparing breakfast when she suddenly noticed that her right hand felt weak. She could move the arm, but precise movements were difficult. Her family also noticed a mild problem finding words.

CASE 02 · INTERNAL CAPSULE

The Pure Hemiparesis

ضعف نصفي نقي بدون علامات قشرية واضحة

A 62-year-old man suddenly develops weakness involving his entire right face, arm, and leg. There are no clear cortical sensory or language abnormalities.

AGE 62 years
ONSET Sudden
DEFICIT Right hemiparesis
KEY CLUE Pure motor pattern

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient suddenly drops a cup from his right hand. Within minutes, his right arm and leg become weak. His family notices that the right side of his lower face also appears weak.

أسقط المريض كوبًا من يده اليمنى بشكل مفاجئ. وخلال دقائق أصبح هناك ضعف في الذراع والساق اليمنى، ولاحظت أسرته أيضًا ضعفًا في الجزء السفلي من الجانب الأيمن للوجه.

13:12

13:15

Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we find?

01 · FACE

Right Lower Facial Weakness

The lower right side of the face is weak with preserved forehead movement.

Clue: this can reflect corticobulbar pathway involvement above the facial nucleus.
02 · ARM

Right Arm Weakness

The right upper limb is significantly weak.

Clue: corticospinal fibers are involved.
03 · LEG

Right Leg Weakness

The right lower limb is also weak.

Clue: face, arm, and leg involvement suggests a compact central motor pathway.
04 · CORTICAL SIGNS

No Major Cortical Features

There is no prominent aphasia, neglect, or cortical sensory deficit.

Clue: this favors a subcortical localization.

CASE 03 · SPINAL CORD

The Level Below the Chest

A 45-year-old man develops progressive weakness in both legs. He reports a clear change in sensation beginning around the lower chest and extending downward.

DEFICIT Bilateral leg weakness
KEY CLUE Sensory level
LOCALIZATION Spinal cord

The patient initially noticed that both legs felt heavy. Over the next several hours, walking became difficult. He then noticed a distinct change in sensation beginning around the lower part of his chest. His arms remain normal.

Motor Localization · الخلاصة

Think Along the Pathway

When localizing a motor lesion, do not stop at “weakness.” Ask where along the motor pathway the problem must be.

Motor Cortex

Weakness can follow a somatotopic pattern and may be accompanied by other cortical findings.

Key clue: cortical signs + selective distribution.

Internal Capsule

Descending motor fibers are tightly packed, producing a dense contralateral motor deficit.

Key clue: face + arm + leg weakness without major cortical signs.

Spinal Cord

Motor and sensory findings can appear below a defined anatomical level.

Key clue: sensory level + bilateral or patterned limb deficits.

The Localization Rule

Distribution matters. Reflexes matter. Associated findings matter. A motor deficit becomes much more informative when you combine all three with the anatomy of the descending motor pathway.
