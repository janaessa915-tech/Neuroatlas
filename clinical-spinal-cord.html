Spinal Cord
Localization

تحديد موضع الآفة في الحبل الشوكي

The spinal cord contains ascending sensory pathways, descending motor pathways, segmental neurons, and autonomic pathways. Because these systems follow organized anatomical patterns, neurological findings can often reveal the level and side of a spinal cord lesion. The most useful clues include a sensory level, upper motor neuron signs below the lesion, segmental findings at the lesion level, and specific patterns of sensory loss.

يحتوي الحبل الشوكي على مسارات حسية صاعدة، ومسارات حركية نازلة، وخلايا عصبية قطعية، ومسارات للجهاز العصبي الذاتي. وبسبب التنظيم التشريحي الدقيق لهذه المسارات، يمكن للأعراض العصبية أن تساعدنا في تحديد مستوى وجانب الإصابة. ومن أهم العلامات: وجود مستوى حسي واضح، وعلامات إصابة العصبون الحركي العلوي أسفل الآفة، وعلامات قطعية عند مستوى الإصابة، وأنماط محددة لفقد الإحساس.

CASE 01 · THORACIC CORD

The Sensory Level

مستوى حسي واضح مع ضعف في الطرفين السفليين

A 42-year-old patient develops progressive weakness in both legs and notices that sensation is different below the level of the lower chest.

AGE 42 years
ONSET Progressive
KEY CLUE Thoracic sensory level
MOTOR Bilateral leg weakness

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient initially notices that both legs feel unusually heavy. Over several days, walking becomes increasingly difficult. The patient also notices reduced sensation beginning around the lower part of the chest and extending downward. There is no facial weakness and no cranial nerve abnormality.

لاحظ المريض في البداية أن ساقيه أصبحتا ثقيلتين بشكل غير معتاد. وخلال عدة أيام أصبحت عملية المشي أكثر صعوبة. كما لاحظ تغيرًا في الإحساس يبدأ تقريبًا من الجزء السفلي من الصدر ويمتد إلى أسفل. ولا يوجد ضعف في الوجه أو أي علامات تخص الأعصاب القحفية.

DAY 01
Both legs begin feeling heavy.
DAY 02
Walking becomes slower and more difficult.
DAY 03
A clear sensory change is noticed below the lower chest.
DAY 04
Neurological examination confirms bilateral leg weakness.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we find?

01 · SENSORY LEVEL

Clear Truncal Sensory Level

Sensation is relatively normal above the lower chest but altered below that level.

Clue: a sensory level is a major clue for spinal cord localization.
02 · MOTOR

Bilateral Leg Weakness

Both lower limbs are weak, while upper limb function remains relatively preserved.

Clue: the distribution points below the cervical cord.
03 · UPPER MOTOR NEURON

Increased Reflexes Below the Lesion

Reflexes in the lower limbs are brisk.

Clue: corticospinal tract involvement can produce UMN signs below a spinal cord lesion.
04 · CRANIAL NERVES

No Cranial Nerve Findings

Eye movements, facial movement, speech, and swallowing are preserved.

Clue: absence of cranial nerve involvement supports a spinal rather than brainstem localization.

01 · Recognize the pattern

Bilateral leg weakness plus a clear truncal sensory level strongly suggests a spinal cord lesion rather than an isolated peripheral nerve lesion.

Motor Both legs
Sensation Defined level
Cranial nerves Normal

02 · Find the sensory level

The sensory change begins around the lower thoracic region. A horizontal sensory boundary on the trunk is particularly useful because spinal sensory pathways are organized by segmental levels.

Above level Relatively preserved
At level Thoracic sensory change
Below level Reduced sensation

03 · Analyze the motor pathway

The corticospinal tract descends through the spinal cord. A lesion in the thoracic cord can therefore produce upper motor neuron weakness in both legs below the lesion.

Upper limbs Relatively preserved
Lower limbs Weakness + brisk reflexes
Tract Corticospinal

04 · Determine the level

Because the upper limbs are preserved and the sensory level is thoracic, the lesion is most consistent with the thoracic spinal cord.

Cervical Less compatible
Thoracic Strongly supported
Lumbar Less compatible with UMN signs

05 · Final localization

The overall pattern is most consistent with a thoracic spinal cord lesion producing bilateral corticospinal dysfunction and a defined sensory level.

Region Thoracic cord
Major tract Corticospinal
Key clue Sensory level

CASE 02 · CERVICAL CORD

The Weak Hands

ضعف اليدين مع علامات إصابة الحبل الشوكي العنقي

A 55-year-old patient develops weakness affecting the hands and arms, followed by difficulty walking and abnormal reflexes in the legs.

AGE 55 years
ONSET Progressive
KEY REGION Cervical cord
KEY CLUE Arm + leg findings

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient initially notices difficulty opening jars and buttoning clothes. Over time, the weakness becomes more noticeable in the hands. The patient later develops stiffness in both legs and difficulty walking. There is no facial weakness, diplopia, or speech disturbance.

لاحظ المريض في البداية صعوبة في فتح البرطمانات وربط أزرار الملابس. ومع مرور الوقت أصبح الضعف أكثر وضوحًا في اليدين. ثم ظهرت صعوبة في المشي مع تيبس في الساقين. ولا يوجد ضعف في الوجه أو ازدواجية في الرؤية أو اضطراب في الكلام.

WEEK 01
Fine hand movements become difficult.
WEEK 03
Grip weakness becomes more noticeable.
WEEK 05
Leg stiffness and gait difficulty develop.
WEEK 06
Examination reveals mixed arm and leg signs.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we find?

01 · HANDS

Hand Weakness

Fine motor tasks are difficult and grip strength is reduced.

Clue: segmental lower motor neuron involvement can occur at the level of a cervical cord lesion.
02 · LEGS

Leg Spasticity

The legs are stiff with brisk reflexes.

Clue: corticospinal tract involvement below the lesion produces upper motor neuron signs.
03 · SENSORY

Upper-Limb Sensory Change

Sensation is altered in the hands and upper limbs.

Clue: segmental sensory findings can help identify the cervical level.
04 · CRANIAL NERVES

No Cranial Nerve Deficit

Eye movements, facial movement, and swallowing remain normal.

Clue: this supports a spinal localization.

Interactive Localization Engine

A cervical cord lesion can create different neurological signs at the level of the lesion and below it.

01 · Recognize the pattern

The combination of hand weakness and upper motor neuron signs in the legs suggests a lesion within the cervical spinal cord.

Arms Weak hands
Legs UMN signs
Brainstem No cranial findings

02 · Analyze the hand findings

A cervical cord lesion can damage neurons or their connections at the affected segment, producing weakness in muscles supplied by that region.

Hands Weakness
Pattern Segmental
Region Cervical

03 · Analyze the legs

Below a cervical cord lesion, descending corticospinal fibers can be disrupted, producing bilateral upper motor neuron signs in the legs.

Reflexes Brisk
Tone Increased

04 · Determine the level

Because both upper and lower limbs are involved, the lesion must be located at or above the cervical enlargement rather than in the thoracic cord.

Cervical Strongly supported
Thoracic Would spare the arms
Lumbar Would not explain hand weakness

05 · Final localization

The combination of hand weakness, upper-limb sensory changes, and bilateral leg upper motor neuron signs is most consistent with a cervical spinal cord lesion.

Region Cervical cord
Upper limbs Segmental involvement
Lower limbs Long-tract involvement

CASE 03 · HEMICORD

The Half-Cord Pattern

نمط نصف الحبل الشوكي

A 36-year-old patient develops weakness and several different patterns of sensory loss affecting one side of the body after a spinal injury.

AGE 36 years
ONSET Acute
KEY PATTERN Unilateral cord findings
KEY CLUE Crossed sensory loss

History · التاريخ المرضي

Following a spinal injury, the patient develops weakness on the right side of the lower body. Vibration and position sense are reduced on the right side, while pain and temperature sensation are reduced on the opposite side beginning several segments below the lesion.

بعد إصابة في العمود الفقري، ظهر لدى المريض ضعف في الجانب الأيمن من الجزء السفلي من الجسم. كما انخفض الإحساس بالاهتزاز ووضعية المفاصل في الجانب الأيمن، بينما انخفض الإحساس بالألم والحرارة في الجانب المقابل بدايةً من عدة قطاعات أسفل مستوى الإصابة.

0 HR
Spinal injury occurs.
1 HR
Right-sided weakness becomes apparent.
2 HR
Right vibration and position sense are reduced.
3 HR
Left pain and temperature sensation become impaired below the lesion.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we find?

01 · MOTOR

Right-Sided Weakness

Weakness is greater on the right side below the level of injury.

Clue: corticospinal fibers are organized within the spinal cord.
02 · DORSAL COLUMN

Right Loss of Vibration + Position

The patient has impaired vibration and proprioception on the right side below the lesion.

Clue: dorsal column pathways ascend ipsilaterally within the spinal cord.
03 · SPINOTHALAMIC

Left Pain + Temperature Loss

Pain and temperature sensation are reduced on the opposite side, beginning below the lesion.

Clue: spinothalamic fibers cross within the spinal cord.
04 · PATTERN

Three-Pathway Combination

Motor, dorsal-column, and spinothalamic findings create a highly specific pattern.

Clue: multiple pathways point toward a hemicord lesion.

Interactive Localization Engine

The key is to understand where each sensory pathway crosses.

01 · Analyze the motor finding

The right-sided weakness below the lesion suggests involvement of the right corticospinal tract within the spinal cord.

Weakness Right side
Pathway Corticospinal
Localization Spinal cord

02 · Analyze vibration and position

The dorsal columns carry vibration, fine touch, and proprioceptive information upward on the same side of the spinal cord.

Loss Right
Modalities Vibration + position
Pathway Dorsal columns

03 · Analyze pain and temperature

Pain and temperature fibers cross within the spinal cord before ascending. Therefore a left-sided loss below the lesion can point toward a right-sided spinal cord lesion.

Loss Left
Modalities Pain + temperature
Crossing Within spinal cord

04 · Determine the side

Right-sided corticospinal and dorsal-column findings together with contralateral pain-temperature loss indicate a right-sided hemicord lesion.

Motor Right
Dorsal column Right
Pain/temp Left

05 · Final localization

The combination of ipsilateral weakness and dorsal-column loss with contralateral pain-temperature loss is classically associated with a spinal cord hemisection pattern.

Region Spinal cord
Side Right hemicord
Pattern Hemicord syndrome

Correct. The dorsal columns carry vibration, proprioception, and fine discriminative touch.

Correct. The dorsal column pathway remains on the same side within the spinal cord before crossing higher in the nervous system.

Correct. The combination of ipsilateral motor and dorsal-column loss with contralateral pain-temperature loss is characteristic of a hemicord pattern.

Clinical Localization Rule · قاعدة مهمة

When localizing a spinal cord lesion, first ask four questions: Is there a sensory level? Are the motor signs upper or lower motor neuron? Which sensory modalities are affected? And are the arms involved? These questions can narrow the lesion to a specific region of the cord before you even identify the exact pathology.

عند تحديد موضع آفة الحبل الشوكي، ابدأ بأربع أسئلة: هل يوجد مستوى حسي؟ هل العلامات الحركية من نوع العصبون الحركي العلوي أم السفلي؟ ما أنواع الإحساس المتأثرة؟ وهل الذراعان متأثران؟ تساعد هذه الأسئلة في تحديد منطقة الحبل الشوكي قبل تحديد المرض نفسه.
