Medulla

The medulla oblongata is the most inferior part of the brainstem. It forms the transition between the brainstem and spinal cord and contains pathways, nuclei, and regulatory networks essential for normal body function.

📍 Where is the Medulla?

The medulla is the lowest part of the brainstem. It lies inferior to the pons and continues caudally into the spinal cord. It is located anterior to the cerebellum. Think of the brainstem as:

Midbrain ↓ Pons ↓ Medulla ↓ Spinal cord
Core Functions

What does the Medulla do?

01 · RESPIRATORY

Breathing

Medullary networks contribute to the generation and regulation of respiratory activity.

02 · CARDIOVASCULAR

Heart & Blood Pressure

Medullary networks participate in autonomic cardiovascular regulation.

03 · REFLEXES

Protective Reflexes

The medulla participates in important reflexes including swallowing and coughing.

Neuroanatomy

Key Structures

Pyramids

The anterior medulla contains the pyramids, which carry descending corticospinal motor fibers. Many of these fibers cross at the pyramidal decussation.

Inferior Olivary Complex

The inferior olive participates in circuits connecting the brainstem and spinal cord with the cerebellum.

Cranial Nerves IX & X

Medullary nuclei are associated with the glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves and participate in swallowing, visceral sensation, and autonomic functions.

Cranial Nerves XI & XII

The medullary region is associated with structures related to the accessory and hypoglossal nerves.

Clinical Connection

Why does the Medulla matter?

Because the medulla contains important pathways and regulatory systems, damage to this region can produce significant neurological effects. The specific symptoms depend on which pathways, nuclei, or blood vessels are affected.
