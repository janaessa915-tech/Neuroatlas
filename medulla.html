Medulla

The medulla oblongata is the lowest part of the brainstem. It connects the pons to the spinal cord and contains important centers involved in breathing, cardiovascular regulation, swallowing, and other essential functions.

📍 Location of the Medulla

The medulla is the lowest portion of the brainstem. It is located below the pons and continues downward into the spinal cord.

It sits near the base of the brain and helps connect the brain with the spinal cord.

What is the Medulla?

The medulla oblongata is one of the three major parts of the brainstem, together with the midbrain and pons.

What does it do?

The medulla contains pathways and neural centers involved in important involuntary functions such as breathing, heart-rate regulation, blood pressure, swallowing, and coughing.

Why is it important?

Because the medulla participates in essential automatic functions, damage to this region can seriously affect the body's ability to maintain normal physiological functions.
