Medulla

The medulla oblongata is the most inferior part of the brainstem. It forms the transition between the brainstem and spinal cord and contains pathways, nuclei, and regulatory centers essential for normal body function.

📍 Where is the Medulla?

The medulla is the lowest part of the brainstem. It lies inferior to the pons and continues caudally until it becomes continuous with the spinal cord near the foramen magnum. It is positioned anterior to the cerebellum. Think of the brainstem as a vertical pathway:

Midbrain ↓ Pons ↓ Medulla ↓ Spinal cord
Core Functions

What does the Medulla do?

01 · AUTONOMIC

Breathing

Networks within the medulla contribute to the generation and regulation of respiratory activity.

02 · CARDIOVASCULAR

Heart & Blood Pressure

Medullary networks participate in cardiovascular regulation, including control of autonomic output.

03 · REFLEXES

Protective Reflexes

Medullary circuits participate in important reflexes including coughing, swallowing, and related protective responses.

Neuroanatomy

Key Structures

Pyramids

The anterior medulla contains the pyramids, which carry descending corticospinal motor fibers. Many of these fibers cross at the pyramidal decussation before continuing in the spinal cord.

Inferior Olivary Complex

The inferior olive participates in circuits connecting the spinal cord and brainstem with the cerebellum, contributing to motor learning and coordination.

Cranial Nerves IX & X

The glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves are associated with medullary nuclei involved in swallowing, visceral sensation, autonomic regulation, and protective reflexes.

Cranial Nerves XI & XII

Medullary structures are associated with the accessory and hypoglossal nerves. CN XII provides motor innervation to the tongue.

Clinical Connection

Why does the Medulla matter?

Because the medulla contains pathways and regulatory systems involved in essential functions, lesions in this region can produce significant neurological effects. One example is lateral medullary syndrome, which can involve structures supplied by branches of the vertebral or posterior inferior cerebellar arterial circulation.
