Medulla

The medulla oblongata is the most inferior part of the brainstem. It connects the pons with the spinal cord and contains important pathways and neural networks involved in breathing, cardiovascular regulation, swallowing, and protective reflexes.

01 · Location

Where is the Medulla?

📍 Brainstem → Medulla

The medulla is the lowest part of the brainstem.

Midbrain ↓ Pons ↓ Medulla ↓ Spinal cord

It lies anterior to the cerebellum and forms the transition between the brainstem and spinal cord.
02 · Functions

What does it do?

01 · RESPIRATION

Breathing

Neural networks in the medulla contribute to the generation and regulation of respiratory activity.

02 · CARDIOVASCULAR

Heart & Blood Pressure

Medullary autonomic networks participate in regulation of cardiovascular activity.

03 · REFLEXES

Protective Reflexes

The medulla participates in important reflexes such as swallowing and coughing.

03 · Anatomy

Important Structures

Pyramids

The anterior medulla contains the pyramids, which carry descending corticospinal motor fibers.

Inferior Olive

The inferior olivary complex participates in connections between the brainstem and cerebellum.

Cranial Nerves IX & X

Medullary nuclei associated with the glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves participate in swallowing and visceral functions.

Cranial Nerves XI & XII

Structures in the medullary region are associated with the accessory and hypoglossal nerves.

05 · Clinical Connection

Why is the Medulla important?

The medulla contains important neural pathways and regulatory networks. Disruption of the medulla can affect breathing, cardiovascular regulation, swallowing, and motor or sensory pathways. The exact effects depend on the structures affected.
