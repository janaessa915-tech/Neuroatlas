Medulla

The medulla oblongata is the inferior portion of the brainstem. It connects the pons to the spinal cord.

It contains important centers involved in: breathing, cardiovascular regulation, blood pressure, and protective reflexes.

Medulla highlighted

📍 Location

The medulla oblongata is the lowest part of the brainstem.

  • Superiorly → Pons
  • Inferiorly → Spinal cord
  • Posteriorly → Fourth ventricle region

🫀 Autonomic Functions

The medulla contains important autonomic centers that help regulate vital functions.

  • Respiratory regulation
  • Heart rate
  • Blood pressure
  • Swallowing
  • Coughing
  • Vomiting
  • Sneezing

🧬 Important Structures

  • Pyramids — contain corticospinal fibers.
  • Olives — associated with the inferior olivary nuclei.
  • Pyramidal decussation — crossing of corticospinal fibers.
  • Important cranial nerve nuclei.

⚡ Clinical Importance

The medulla contains pathways and centers responsible for vital functions.

This makes the medulla one of the most clinically important parts of the brainstem.
