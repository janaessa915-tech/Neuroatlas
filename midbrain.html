Midbrain

الدماغ الأوسط

The midbrain is the uppermost part of the brainstem. It plays important roles in eye movement, visual and auditory reflexes, motor control, and communication between different regions of the nervous system.

📍 Location

The midbrain is located between the diencephalon and the pons. It forms the upper part of the brainstem.

👁️ Visual Reflexes

The superior colliculus helps coordinate reflexive movements of the eyes and head in response to visual stimuli.

👂 Auditory Processing

The inferior colliculus is an important relay in the auditory pathway and contributes to auditory reflexes.

🚶 Motor Control

The midbrain contains the substantia nigra and other structures that participate in movement and motor control.

🔗 Major Structures

Important structures include the cerebral peduncles, tectum, tegmentum, superior colliculus, inferior colliculus, and substantia nigra.

🩺 Clinical Relevance

Midbrain injury can affect eye movements, motor function, consciousness, and communication between sensory and motor pathways.

NeuroAtlas Insight

Although the midbrain is relatively small, it contains important pathways and nuclei involved in movement, sensory processing, reflexes, and alertness.
