Brainstem

Midbrain

الدماغ الأوسط

The midbrain is the uppermost part of the brainstem. It helps coordinate eye movements, visual and auditory reflexes, motor control, and communication between higher brain regions and the brainstem.

📍 Location

The midbrain lies between the diencephalon above and the pons below, forming the upper portion of the brainstem.

👁️ Visual Reflexes

The superior colliculus is involved in orienting movements of the eyes and head toward important visual stimuli.

👂 Auditory Reflexes

The inferior colliculus is an important relay in auditory pathways and contributes to auditory reflexes.

🚶 Motor Control

The midbrain contains structures involved in movement, including the substantia nigra and connections with basal ganglia circuits.

🔗 Major Structures

Important midbrain structures include the cerebral peduncles, tectum, tegmentum, superior colliculi, inferior colliculi, and substantia nigra.

🩺 Clinical Relevance

Damage to the midbrain can affect eye movements, motor function, consciousness, and pathways carrying sensory and motor information.

NeuroAtlas Insight

The midbrain is a major communication hub. Although relatively small, it contains pathways and nuclei involved in movement, sensory processing, reflexes, and maintaining alertness.
