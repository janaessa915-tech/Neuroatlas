Visual
Localization

تحديد موضع الآفة في المسار البصري

Visual localization is based on one powerful principle: the pattern of visual field loss tells us where the visual pathway has been interrupted. The pathway begins at the retina, travels through the optic nerves, meets at the optic chiasm, continues through the optic tracts and lateral geniculate bodies, and finally reaches the visual cortex through the optic radiations.

تحديد موضع الآفة في المسار البصري يعتمد بشكل أساسي على شكل فقدان المجال البصري. يبدأ المسار من الشبكية، ثم العصب البصري، والتصالب البصري، ثم السبيل البصري، والإشعاعات البصرية، حتى يصل إلى القشرة البصرية في الفص القذالي.

CASE 01 · OPTIC NERVE

The One-Eye Blind Spot

فقدان الرؤية في عين واحدة

A 42-year-old woman suddenly notices that vision in her right eye has become severely reduced. Vision in the left eye remains normal.

AGE 42 years
ONSET Sudden
KEY FINDING Monocular visual loss
LOCALIZATION Right optic nerve

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient reports that objects appear normal when viewed with her left eye, but vision is markedly reduced when she closes the left eye. There is no clear loss of the temporal or nasal half of vision in both eyes.

تذكر المريضة أن الأشياء تبدو طبيعية عند استخدام العين اليسرى، لكن الرؤية تنخفض بشكل واضح عند إغلاق العين اليسرى. لا يوجد نمط واضح لفقدان نصف المجال البصري في كلتا العينين.

09:10
Vision is initially normal.
09:25
Patient notices blurred vision in the right eye.
09:40
Right-eye visual loss becomes obvious.
10:00
Left-eye vision remains preserved.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we find?

01 · RIGHT EYE

Marked Monocular Visual Loss

The right eye has substantially reduced vision.

Clue: isolated monocular loss points toward the visual pathway before the optic chiasm.
02 · LEFT EYE

Left Vision Preserved

The left eye has relatively normal visual function.

Clue: both eyes are not affected in the same visual field pattern.
03 · VISUAL FIELD

No Homonymous Pattern

There is no matching field deficit affecting the same side of visual space in both eyes.

Clue: this makes a post-chiasmal lesion less likely.
04 · LOCALIZATION

Pre-Chiasmal Pathway

The pattern is most consistent with a lesion affecting the right optic nerve or retina.

Clue: localization begins by asking whether one eye or both eyes are affected.

CASE 02 · OPTIC CHIASM

The Missing Sides

فقدان المجالين الصدغيين

A 51-year-old woman notices that she repeatedly bumps into objects on both sides of her surroundings. She can still see objects directly in front of her.

AGE 51 years
ONSET Gradual
KEY FINDING Bitemporal loss
LOCALIZATION Optic chiasm

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient reports difficulty noticing objects approaching from either side. When tested formally, the temporal portions of both visual fields are reduced while central vision remains relatively preserved.

تذكر المريضة أنها تجد صعوبة في ملاحظة الأشياء القادمة من جانبيها. وعند فحص المجال البصري، يظهر فقدان في الأجزاء الصدغية من المجال البصري لكلتا العينين، بينما تظل الرؤية المركزية محفوظة نسبيًا.

Weeks
Patient begins noticing objects from the sides less often.
Later
She starts bumping into objects beside her.
Exam
Temporal visual fields are reduced bilaterally.
Result
Bitemporal hemianopic pattern is identified.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we find?

01 · RIGHT VISUAL FIELD

Temporal Field Loss

The temporal field of the right eye is reduced.

Clue: temporal visual information comes from the nasal retina.
02 · LEFT VISUAL FIELD

Temporal Field Loss

The temporal field of the left eye is also reduced.

Clue: the same side of visual space is not simply lost in both eyes; the temporal fields are affected.
03 · CENTRAL VISION

Relatively Preserved

Central visual function remains relatively better preserved.

Clue: the pattern is not typical of complete loss of one optic nerve.
04 · LOCALIZATION

Optic Chiasm

The pattern strongly suggests involvement of the optic chiasm, where nasal retinal fibers cross.

Clue: bitemporal hemianopia is a classic chiasmal localization.

01 · Recognize the field pattern

Both temporal visual fields are affected. This is called a bitemporal hemianopic pattern.

02 · Which retinal fibers carry temporal-field information?

The temporal visual field projects onto the nasal retina. The nasal retinal fibers are the fibers that cross at the chiasm.

Temporal field Projects to nasal retina
Nasal retinal fibers Cross
Key structure Optic chiasm

03 · Identify the crossing point

The optic chiasm is the point where nasal retinal fibers from the two eyes cross to the opposite side.

Right nasal fibers Cross
Left nasal fibers Cross
Crossing Optic chiasm

04 · Determine the pathway level

Because both temporal fields are affected, the lesion is best localized to the optic chiasm rather than one optic nerve or a post-chiasmal structure.

Optic nerve Usually monocular
Optic chiasm Strongly supported
Optic tract Usually homonymous

05 · Final localization

The bitemporal pattern localizes to the optic chiasm, where the nasal retinal fibers cross.

Pattern Bitemporal hemianopia
Structure Optic chiasm
Fiber group Nasal retinal fibers

CASE 03 · RETROCHIASMAL

The Missing Half

فقدان نصف المجال البصري في العينين

A 67-year-old man suddenly notices that he cannot see objects on the left side of his surroundings. Testing shows loss of the left half of the visual field in both eyes.

AGE 67 years
ONSET Sudden
KEY FINDING Left homonymous hemianopia
LOCALIZATION Right retrochiasmal pathway

History · التاريخ المرضي

The patient reports suddenly missing objects and people on his left. When reading, he sometimes loses the beginning of a line. Formal visual-field testing demonstrates loss of the left visual field in both eyes.

يذكر المريض أنه بدأ فجأة في عدم ملاحظة الأشياء والأشخاص الموجودين على الجانب الأيسر منه. وعند القراءة يجد أحيانًا صعوبة في رؤية بداية السطر. ويُظهر فحص المجال البصري فقدان الجانب الأيسر من المجال البصري في كلتا العينين.

13:40
Sudden visual disturbance begins.
13:45
Patient notices objects missing on the left.
14:00
Visual-field testing reveals left-sided field loss.
14:15
Both eyes show the same left visual-field deficit.
Neurological Examination · الفحص العصبي

What do we find?

01 · RIGHT EYE

Left Visual Field Loss

The left half of the visual field is absent from the right eye.

Clue: the deficit is described by visual space rather than by one eye alone.
02 · LEFT EYE

Left Visual Field Loss

The same left-sided visual field is affected in the left eye.

Clue: matching visual-field loss in both eyes is homonymous.
03 · FIELD PATTERN

Left Homonymous Hemianopia

The patient has loss of the same side of visual space in both eyes.

Clue: this pattern indicates a post-chiasmal lesion.
04 · SIDE

Right Retrochiasmal Pathway

A left homonymous defect generally indicates a lesion on the right side behind the optic chiasm.

Clue: post-chiasmal pathways represent the opposite visual field.
05 · POSSIBLE LEVEL

Optic Tract → Radiations → Cortex

Several structures can produce a homonymous defect, so additional clinical findings may be needed to identify the exact level.

Clue: visual-field pattern localizes the side, while associated findings help determine the precise structure.
06 · HIGHER CORTICAL CLUE

Consider the Occipital Cortex

If the deficit is isolated and highly congruous, an occipital localization becomes particularly important.

Clue: the visual cortex is located in the occipital lobe.

Clinical Takeaway · الخلاصة السريرية

Visual localization can often be approached with three questions: Is one eye affected or both? Which part of the visual field is lost? And does the pattern indicate a pre-chiasmal, chiasmal, or post-chiasmal lesion?

يمكن تحديد موضع الآفة في المسار البصري من خلال ثلاثة أسئلة أساسية: هل تتأثر عين واحدة أم كلتا العينين؟ ما الجزء المفقود من المجال البصري؟ وهل يشير النمط إلى آفة قبل التصالب البصري أم في التصالب أم بعده؟
